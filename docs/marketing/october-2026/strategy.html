Editorial decision

Use two weekly slots per product on its own account or an audience-matched channel: one demonstration or carousel, plus one useful, saveable static post. That creates eight slots per product, with four static assets, two edited video cuts and two carousel arrangements. Twelve unrelated daily posts on the studio account would dilute the story. Use the separate three-post weekly studio rotation instead: twelve posts, one feature per product over the month.

If product accounts do not exist, prioritize the studio rotation; the 96-slot library is optional inventory for product channels, not a requirement to launch twelve accounts. The initial 12:30 and 18:00 São Paulo times are operational defaults. Revisit them after the first week using actual audience geography and account insights.

Audience groups and creative roles

  • Everyday care and meaningful moments: Giftly, Memories, Hooray, zuzu and Soooon. Warm language, specific everyday triggers, clear product pixels; no fabricated testimonials.
  • Useful clarity: Ground Control, Poof, Toc Toc and My Renewals. Show the information users review before making a decision. Avoid guaranteed savings, blanket deletion advice or promises of developer productivity.
  • Study practice: Brainfold. Start with course material and show flashcards, quizzes and contextual help. Avoid guaranteed exam results.
  • Exploration and play: Sundust and TumTum. Lead with real gameplay. Sundust emphasizes player freedom; TumTum retains the approved tactile paper world and parent-facing voice.

What to validate before changing live listings

The store evidence records observed listing titles, descriptions and versions in checked storefronts. Live App Store keyword fields are not public; the draft is not a claim that current keywords were inspected. Review signed-in App Store Connect fields and Search Ads search-term evidence before replacing metadata.

  • Giftly: test the “remember dates + save gift ideas” promise against a reminder-only opening.
  • Memories: compare the current baby-focused acquisition message with the broader monthly-film angle. Keep the original baby-focused listing until conversion and activation evidence support broadening it.
  • Brainfold: test the “turn material into practice” opening. AI alone is a weak differentiator: Quizlet already markets AI study tools.
  • zuzu: emphasize coordinated everyday care. Huckleberry emphasizes sleep predictions; this campaign does not make medical or sleep-outcome claims.
  • Poof: emphasize reviewable developer clutter. DaisyDisk already visualizes disk usage; do not position visualization alone as unique.
  • Toc Toc, Soooon, Ground Control and unverified Poof: use “Explore the project” and preserve current availability. Do not send viewers to a promised public download without release evidence.

The four-week learning loop

  1. Week 1 · Establish the promise. Lead with the first video and opening static benefit. Record source-tagged landing visits, store taps, product-page views and available first-use events. Export the prior 28-day store baseline before launch; no baseline has been invented here.
  2. Week 2 · Show the mechanism. Publish the first carousel and detail post. Compare saves, profile taps and product-page engagement. Answer common questions in future copy; do not rewrite the whole campaign from a small sample.
  3. Week 3 · Test a second trigger. Use the alternate video and its matched post. Compare median watch time, completion, outbound clicks and installation conversion where observable. Viewers, landing visits and installs are separate denominators.
  4. Week 4 · Reinforce useful detail. Use the second carousel and saveable closing post. Keep strong material for the next month. Report results by product, channel and creative rather than combining unrelated audiences.

Store artwork tests should isolate the opening benefit or first screenshot while holding the rest steady. Use Apple Product Page Optimization when eligible. Use its actual confidence/readiness reporting; do not promise a fixed uplift or force a winner with little traffic.

Production and measurement

Forty-two primary Apple-store approval images cover ten products; six extra zuzu images target Google Play with native Android captures and an Android handset frame; four Hooray images target the web. TumTum’s store collection is preserved. Twenty videos are edited walkthroughs using real captured screens; four reuse real recorded gameplay from Sundust and TumTum. These are organic social videos, not a claim that every file meets App Store preview submission rules. Google Play art follows Google’s preview-asset guidance. Store sizes follow Apple’s screenshot specifications; eighteen additional iPad images use native iPad captures from Giftly, Soooon, Brainfold and zuzu. iPhone and iPad compositions use original PNG bezels from Apple Design Resources. Standalone bezel artwork remains in a private local cache; only finished product compositions are exported.

Every reel has MP4, SRT and VTT outputs. English copy is outside app pixels, so overlays and captions can be translated independently. ElevenLabs credit is exhausted: new narrations remain scripts, while TumTum retains its existing approved voice. Draft music for the other products is generated procedurally by the local renderer; no unlicensed stock track is included.

Calendar rows include copy, asset paths, time zone and UTM links. Instagram/TikTok use link-in-bio copy; update the destination for the active featured product when publishing. YouTube video descriptions include the tracked URL. Do not paste internal asset paths into published captions.

Approval sequence: review English claims and creative → confirm current signed-in metadata and device variants → translate from the approved reference → schedule on chosen accounts → deploy the site or submit to stores only with explicit authorization. These materials are drafts for this sequence.
